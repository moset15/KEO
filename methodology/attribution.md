# Observation, assessment and attribution

**Observation** records what can be directly inspected: a public statement, a document difference or a reproducible posting pattern. Record the source, date, scope and collection limitations.

**Assessment** explains what those observations support. Consider ordinary explanations such as syndication, satire, legitimate pseudonymity, shared news triggers, corrections and transcription errors. State uncertainty and distinguish a plausible interpretation from a documented fact.

**Attribution** connects responsibility or control to an operator. It requires substantially stronger evidence, such as authenticated control records or a transparent, well-supported investigation. Political benefit, copied imagery, shared wording, language, nationality or matching indicators alone do not identify an operator.

When discussing a platform or research publisher's attribution, name the publisher and the scope of its finding. Do not present a reported attribution as an independent KEO observation. Findings about another country do not establish activity in Kenya.

KEO does not identify private people behind accounts, infer voter affiliations, track private locations or automatically accuse anyone of fraud. An arithmetic discrepancy is a data-quality issue requiring review. An unverified claim remains unverified. No model response can substitute for the stronger evidence needed to name a responsible person or organisation.
