# Threat Observatory content notes

The fourteen entries in `data/threats/taxonomy.json` are original editorial reference guidance for the MVP taxonomy specified in the supplied brief. Every entry has the status **Reference technique**. None is an incident record, a finding that a technique has occurred in Kenya, or an attribution to a person, party, organisation or country.

All examples are explicitly labelled **Hypothetical example**. No actual case, report date, quotation, reach estimate or impact measure has been invented. The interface should label this section “Hypothetical example”, rather than presenting these illustrations as “Known examples”. A real example can be added only with its own evidence record, exact publication URL and date, and an appropriate redistribution check.

Each threshold separates three questions:

- **Observation:** what is directly documented, with the original public material, dates and collection limits.
- **Assessment:** what that evidence supports, accounting for ordinary alternative explanations and remaining uncertainty.
- **Attribution:** what separate, stronger evidence connects responsibility or control to an operator. Repeated content, political benefit, language, appearance and account similarity do not establish responsibility.

The `reportSourceIds` fields resolve to source registry entries. They identify publishers to consult, not specific ingested reports, and do not substantiate an observed Kenyan case. The separate curated evidence catalogue contains an attributed September 2026 Anthropic Kenya case. It preserves the publisher’s limitations, including no evidence of government involvement and unresolved operator identity; it is not independent KEO verification or a basis for labelling all fourteen techniques observed.

Kenya relevance describes a possible verification use, with explicit limits. No entry provides current voting instructions, polling estimates, current incidents or official results. Procedural and result claims require current applicable primary records; cached legal context alone cannot verify a new notice. Public research concerning a different country does not establish Kenyan activity.

Content deliberately distinguishes false claims from missing evidence, criticism from harassment, pseudonymity from fabricated identity, ordinary shared messaging from deceptive coordination, and mistakes from fraud. Synthetic-media labels require credible supporting evidence; a generic detector score or visual oddity is insufficient. Personal identifiers, private communications, target lists and inferred political affiliations are outside this taxonomy's purpose.
