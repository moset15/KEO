# Threat taxonomy

The Threat Observatory contains the 14 reference techniques requested in the MVP brief, from fake grassroots personas through manipulated election-result claims. The labels organise verification questions; they are not a live threat feed, a prevalence ranking or proof that any technique has occurred in Kenya.

Every entry has the status **Reference technique**, observable signals, an explicitly hypothetical illustration, evidence thresholds, source-directory references and cautious Kenya relevance. Signals are reasons to investigate, not an automatic classification rule. Each threshold separates observation, assessment and attribution and identifies common limits.

“Sources to consult” links resolve to the source registry. They are not citations to specific ingested public reports. No September 2026 Anthropic report or Kenya finding has been assumed. A real case or related report requires a separate evidence record with an exact publication URL, dates, source context and redistribution terms.

Before publishing a case, establish what was directly observed, what conclusion is supported and what remains uncertain. Verify material context and independent corroboration. Keep impact, reach, coordination and responsibility as separate claims, each with its own evidence. Do not publish personal target lists or infer political affiliations.

Corrections and taxonomy changes belong in versioned data with reviewed explanations. See [content notes](../docs/threat-content-notes.md), [evidence](evidence.md) and [attribution](attribution.md) for the editorial rules.
